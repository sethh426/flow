# Flow sales operating system

Research review: October 15, 2026 (Indianapolis). Owner direction: learn advanced sales and make the learning operational. This is a durable project reference, not a claim of model retraining, customer demand or completed integrations.

## Commercial thesis

Earn attention by helping a specific buyer finish a task. The ten free local workflows are a proof surface. A paid offer, once technically verified, is a separately scoped setup, connection to existing systems and dependable operation of a recurring workflow. One useful export is not necessarily an ongoing business: learn whether the task repeats and where manual exports stop being enough.

Use two acquisition routes. Demand capture follows an original, current request for an implementer or a named project. Product discovery lets a relevant team try a free workflow with no conversation prerequisite. A diagnosis and paid scope follow an engaged buyer's own account of the process and its consequences.

## Source-based principles and limits

- [Gong 2025 cold-email analysis](https://www.gong.io/blog/does-cold-email-even-work-any-more-heres-what-the-data-says), reviewed Oct 15, 2026: over 28 million observed emails; pitches and jargon correlated with weaker replies, shorter emails with more replies. Use buyer language and one concrete result. This is observational data from Gong customers and a promotional article, not a causal test or Flow benchmark.
- [Gong CTA analysis](https://www.gong.io/blog/this-surprising-cold-email-cta-will-help-you-book-a-lot-more-meetings), reviewed Oct 15, 2026: 304,174 emails, measuring meetings within ten days and excluding negative replies. Interest asks performed better in cold email; specific meeting requests worked better after engagement. Use a small first decision; the self-serve workflow link is a Flow experiment, not a proven universal CTA.
- [6sense 2025 Buyer Experience study](https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/buyer-experience-report-2025/), reviewed Oct 15, 2026: nearly 4,000 B2B buyers, many forming shortlists before seller contact; 79% initiated engagement. Give buyers public, useful demonstrations before requesting time. Its sample required purchases of at least $25,000, median $200,000–$300,000, so the percentages cannot forecast a small agency's Flow purchase.
- [Huthwaite SPIN](https://www.huthwaiteinternational.com/spin-methodology) and [Winning by Design SPICED](https://winningbydesign.com/resources/blueprints/the-spiced-framework/), reviewed Oct 15, 2026: diagnose situation, pain, impact, desired payoff, critical event and decision path after engagement. These are vendor-originated frameworks, not an excuse to send a cold questionnaire or infer pain from a website.
- [ProductLed's PQL framework](https://productled.com/blog/how-to-define-pqls), reviewed Oct 15, 2026: distinguish buyer fit, meaningful product value and a hand-raising signal. A signup is not qualification. Flow's current local catalog has no behavioral telemetry; never claim to know who used an export without evidence.
- [Google Postmaster guidance](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/14668346?hl=en), reviewed Oct 15, 2026: authentication and delivery-error dashboards are delayed, limited to personal Gmail recipients and can have low-volume gaps. SENT and absence of a bounce do not prove inbox delivery.

The owner reports roughly one human reply from prior outreach. A complete campaign denominator has not been reconciled. Observed failures included bounced published addresses, fit without active demand, generic offers and one greeting-only send. Reply count alone cannot identify which caused silence.

## Prioritize opportunities

Record the original publication date and exact evidence, not merely a thread's latest reply. Rank: (1) explicit current paid implementation request matching a verified Flow capability, (2) a workflow user requesting connection or customization, (3) a current operational initiative with a relevant decision owner, (4) a public recurring process with no buying signal. An employee vacancy does not imply willingness to hire a contractor. Record required tools, proof, timeline, payment conditions, actual contact route and why Seth is credible. Exclude previously contacted, bounced or opted-out people and companies.

Before each proposed contact, answer: Why this buyer? Why this workflow? Why now? What can we show that actually works? When the answer to buying intent is unknown, say so internally and treat the offer as a low-pressure usefulness test.

## Package a demonstrable offer

For every free workflow define sample input, actual output, useful result, limitations and exact time/effort to first result. Show a real artifact, never invented time savings, customer endorsements or integrations. Minimize data exposure; synthetic examples should allow evaluation without uploading confidential client files.

A first contact should have one verified observation, one relevant demonstrated result, a truthful way to try it and one easy next action. The message must make sense without a reply. Avoid an all-in-one pitch, unsupported diagnosis or meeting demand. Follow the original buyer's required application route when responding to an explicit listing. Never link an undeployed catalog as if public. Respect all sender, deduplication, suppression, opt-out and full-message safeguards. No automatic replies or follow-ups are authorized by this playbook.

Example positioning for a relevant production team: show how a supplied revision list becomes a dated, assigned task CSV. This is a proposed use case, not proof their present system is broken. The paid hypothesis is connecting an approved revision handoff to their existing apps only after the capability and scope are verified.

## Diagnose after engagement

Capture SPICED in the buyer's language, over the conversation rather than as a first-email survey:

| Field | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Situation | Actual tools, owners, volume and one representative sanitized process |
| Pain | Which step the buyer says creates rework or delay |
| Impact | Frequency and consequence measured from buyer data, not invented ROI |
| Critical event | A real reason and date to change, if one exists |
| Decision | Who approves access and spend, proof needed and procurement steps |

Confirm the problem back before prescribing. If there is no material problem, paid outcome or decision path, leave them with the free tool. A polite response is a conversation, not a qualified deal.

## Handle objections without pressure

- Already using Asana/ClickUp: find out whether the current handoff works; demonstrate only a confirmed gap.
- Cannot share client files: use synthetic local examples; document real data access and retention before asking for permissions.
- No time for a call: offer a self-serve sample and brief written explanation.
- Free export is enough: count that as a successful free outcome; do not force an upgrade.
- What will it save: calculate from their baseline during a measured pilot, not a generic ROI promise.
- Can you build this next week: commit only after requirements, access, dependencies and delivery capability are known.

A paid scope should name one result, supported apps, permissions, acceptance checks, customer responsibilities, exclusions, price and terms. Advance only on evidence: explicit desired outcome, decision owner, agreed next action. Agreement is not cash received.

## Measurement and experimentation

Track cohorts by original lead source and offer variant: researched -> qualified -> attempted -> final failure/unresolved/no observed failure -> human response -> positive response -> first useful artifact -> repeat use -> request for connected work -> agreed scope -> cash received. Distinguish opt-outs and automated messages from human commercial responses. Do not use opens as the deciding measure.

Interpret failures separately: high bounce rate suggests contact sourcing; little response with low observed failures might reflect inbox placement, fit, timing or message; response without first use suggests friction or mismatch; first use without repeat suggests limited recurring value; repeat without paid demand may mean free already solves the job; paid interest without close calls for examining decision, security, scope, price and urgency.

Change one major variable at a time in small, labeled experiments: source type, single-workflow result, proof format or first ask. Hold sender and verification quality steady. Compare positive responses, useful first outputs and paid conversations after a reasonable observation window. Do not scale weak outreach to fill a quota. Industry datasets are directional; Flow's measured behavior overrides them.

## Assistant practice

Write an internal one-line rationale before preparing outreach: “This team publicly does X; the verified workflow produces Y; active demand evidence is Z or unknown; the next useful action is A.” Show the output before claiming value. Do not imply I have permanently retrained my model or earned sales mastery through reading; persist the rules here, apply them in future work and update them against real buyer behavior.
